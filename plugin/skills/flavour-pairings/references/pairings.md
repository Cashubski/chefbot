# Flavour pairings

## Method

- **Match or contrast.** Matching deepens (sweet potato with maple, mushroom with thyme). Contrast lifts (pork with apple, fried fish with vinegar, chocolate with salt). Most good dishes do both: a rich base, one contrasting note.
- **Five tastes plus fat and texture.** Salt, sour, sweet, bitter, umami. Fat carries aroma and softens bitterness; texture (crunch, cream, chew) is half of what people call flavour.
- **The five fixes for a flat dish, in order:** salt (most dishes are under-salted), acid (lemon, vinegar, yoghurt, tomato), a touch of sweet (sugar, honey, roasted vegetables), heat (chilli, pepper, mustard, ginger), crunch or contrast (toasted nuts, breadcrumbs, raw herbs, pickles).
- **Shared aroma.** Ingredients that share volatile compounds taste right together: basil and tomato, strawberry and basil, lamb and rosemary, pear and blue cheese, coffee and cardamom.
- **What grows together goes together.** Pair within a region's season and pantry (tomato, olive oil, basil; lime, coriander, chilli; dill, sour cream, potato) and the dish coheres.
- **Two or three leads.** One anchor, one supporting flavour, one contrast. Everything else is seasoning.
