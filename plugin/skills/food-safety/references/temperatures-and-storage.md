# Temperatures, times and storage

## Safe internal temperatures (probe in the thickest part, away from bone)

| Food | UK FSA guidance | USDA | Cue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Whole chicken, turkey, duck; portions on the bone | 75 °C for 30 s (or 70 °C for 2 min) | 74 °C (165 °F) | juices run clear, no pink at the bone; temperature is the test |
| Minced meat, burgers, sausages, meatballs, kebabs | 75 °C | 71 °C (160 °F) beef and pork mince; 74 °C poultry mince | no pink in the centre for mince |
| Pork chops, loin, roasts | 75 °C (FSA); many cooks rest at 63 °C | 63 °C (145 °F) + 3 min rest | may be slightly pink at 63 °C |
| Beef and lamb steaks, roasts, whole muscle | surface seared; interior to taste | 63 °C (145 °F) + 3 min rest for medium-rare | rare 50–52 °C, medium-rare 55–57, medium 60–63, well done 70+ |
| Fish | 63 °C | 63 °C (145 °F) | flakes easily, opaque; tuna and salmon often served lower by choice |
| Prawns, lobster, crab | opaque and firm | 63 °C | shells pink, flesh opaque |
| Mussels, clams, oysters (cooked) | until shells open; discard any that stay shut | - | |
| Eggs | until white and yolk are set, for vulnerable groups; UK Lion-mark eggs can be eaten runny by all | 71 °C (160 °F) for dishes; cook until firm | |
| Egg dishes (quiche, frittata) | 72 °C | 71 °C (160 °F) | centre set, no wobble |
| Stuffing (inside or alongside a bird) | 75 °C | 74 °C (165 °F) | cook stuffing separately if the bird is large |
| Leftovers and reheated food | 75 °C, steaming hot throughout | 74 °C (165 °F) | |
| Ham, cooked (reheated) | 75 °C | 60 °C (140 °F) if pre-cooked, 74 °C if not | |
| Bread (for doneness, not safety) | 90–96 °C | - | hollow sound |
| Caramel / sugar stages | thread 110, soft ball 115, hard ball 125, hard crack 150, caramel 170 °C | | |
| Deep frying oil | 170–190 °C | | |

Resting: large roasts rise 3–5 °C while resting; take them out just under target.

## The 2-hour rule and cooling

- Perishable food should not sit between 8 °C and 63 °C (the "danger zone"; USDA says 4–60 °C) for more than 2 hours in total, or 1 hour if the room is above 32 °C. Anything left out overnight is thrown away, including pizza and rice.
- Cool cooked food within 1–2 hours before refrigerating: spread it out in shallow containers, stand the pot in cold water, do not put a large hot pot in the fridge.
- Rice and pasta: cool within an hour, fridge no more than one day, reheat until steaming; spores of *Bacillus cereus* survive cooking and grow at room temperature.

| Food | Fridge | Freezer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw poultry | 1–2 days (use by date) | 12 months |
| Raw mince, sausages | 1–2 days | 3–4 months |
| Raw steaks, chops, joints | 3–5 days | 6–12 months |
| Raw fish and shellfish | 1–2 days | 3–6 months |
| Cooked meat, poultry, fish dishes | 2 days (FSA), up to 3–4 days (USDA) | 2–6 months |
| Cooked rice, pasta | 1 day | 1 month |
| Soups, stews | 2–3 days | 2–3 months |
| Cooked vegetables | 3–4 days | 10–12 months |
| Opened deli meat | 3 days | 1–2 months |
| Hard cheese (opened) | 3–4 weeks | 6 months (texture suffers) |
| Soft cheese (opened) | 1 week | not recommended |
| Milk (opened) | 3 days after opening, by date | 3 months |
| Eggs | by the best-before date; 3–5 weeks | not in shell; whites and yolks 12 months |
| Butter | 1–2 months | 6–9 months |
| Opened jar sauces (pesto, passata) | 3–5 days | 3 months |

| Cakes with cream or custard | 2–3 days | 1 month |

"Use by" is a safety date: do not eat after it. "Best before" is quality: use your senses.

## Defrosting

- In the fridge: safest. Allow 10–12 hours per kilogram for a joint; a whole chicken 24 hours, a large turkey 2–3 days.
- Cold water: in a sealed bag, change the water every 30 minutes, cook straight away.
- Microwave: cook immediately afterwards.
- Never at room temperature or in hot water. Never refreeze raw food that has been defrosted unless it has been cooked first.
- Cooking from frozen: fine for most vegetables, prawns, fish fillets and mince; add about 50 % to the time and check the internal temperature. Not for whole birds or large joints.

## Reheating

- Reheat once. Until steaming hot throughout: 75 °C for 30 s (USDA 74 °C). Stir halfway in a microwave; cover to steam.
- Gravy, sauces and soups: bring to a rolling boil.
- Cooked rice: within 24 hours, once, until steaming; do not reheat twice.

## When to throw it away

- Any cooked food out more than 2 hours; any raw meat or fish out more than 2 hours.
- Bulging or leaking tins; cloudy liquid in a pickled jar; mould on soft foods, bread, jam, yoghurt, soft cheese, cooked food (cut 2–3 cm around mould only on hard cheese and firm vegetables).
- Slimy, sticky or sour-smelling meat or fish; eggs that float in water (old) or smell when cracked.
- Shellfish that do not open when cooked, or are open and do not close when tapped before cooking.
- Rice that has sat warm or out overnight, even if it smells fine.

## Vulnerable groups (pregnancy, under fives, over sixty-fives, weakened immunity)

- Cook eggs until set unless the eggs carry a national safe-egg mark (UK Lion); avoid raw egg in mousses, homemade mayonnaise, tiramisu.
- Avoid unpasteurised milk and cheese, mould-ripened soft cheese (brie, camembert) and blue cheese unless cooked until steaming, pâté (including vegetable), raw or cured meat, raw shellfish, raw sprouts, smoked fish in pregnancy (UK advice has varied; cooked is safe).
- Honey: not for babies under one year.
- Reheated food must reach 75 °C throughout; avoid buffet food that has stood out.

## Hygiene basics

- Wash hands 20 seconds before cooking and after raw meat, eggs or the bin.
- Separate boards and knives for raw meat; wash with hot soapy water.
- Do not wash raw chicken: it spreads bacteria; cooking kills them.
- Fridge at 0–5 °C; raw meat covered on the bottom shelf.
- Tea towels and sponges carry bacteria; change them often.
