# Recipe checklist and failure diagnosis

## 1. Quantities and ratios

- **Salt, savoury:** 1 to 1.5 percent of the total weight for stews, sauces, braises, mince (5 to 7 g per 500 g, about 1 to 1¼ tsp fine salt). Brines 5 to 6 percent; pasta water 1 percent; bread dough 2 percent of flour weight. A recipe with no salt in a savoury dish is a finding.
- **Pastry (shortcrust):** flour 2 : fat 1 : water about ½, by weight. **Rough puff:** flour 1 : fat 0.75 to 1.
- **Sponge (pound cake):** equal weights flour, sugar, butter, egg. **Victoria sponge:** weigh the eggs, match the rest. **Muffins:** flour 2 : liquid 2 : egg 1 : fat 1 by volume.
- **Bread:** hydration 60 to 75 percent of flour weight; instant yeast 1 to 1.5 percent (5 to 7 g per 500 g); salt 2 percent. **Pizza:** 60 to 65 percent hydration.
- **Baking powder:** 1 tsp per 100 to 125 g flour. **Bicarbonate:** ¼ tsp per 125 g flour, and only with an acid (buttermilk, yoghurt, cocoa, honey). Too much leavening: soapy taste, coarse crumb, collapse.
- **Custard:** 1 egg (or 2 yolks) per 100 to 125 ml liquid for a set; crème anglaise 4 yolks per 500 ml. **Panna cotta:** 1 leaf gelatine per 100 to 125 ml.
- **Rice, absorption:** long grain 1 : 1.5 water by volume (basmati 1 : 1.25 after soaking); risotto 1 : 4 to 5 stock; **pilaf** 1 : 1.5. **Couscous** 1 : 1.2 boiling liquid. **Quinoa** 1 : 1.75. **Dried pasta:** 80 to 100 g per person.
- **Vinaigrette:** 3 oil : 1 acid. **Brine for quick pickles:** 1 vinegar : 1 water : 1 tbsp sugar and 1 tsp salt per 250 ml.
- **Stock to grain in soups:** at least 1 litre per 100 g grain or pulse, or it becomes a stew.
- **Sugar in bread or enriched dough:** above 10 percent of flour slows yeast; increase yeast or time.
- **Meat per person:** 150 to 200 g boneless, 250 to 350 g bone in. **Vegetables as a side:** 80 to 100 g.
- Check that the numbers are **internally consistent**: four eggs and 100 g flour is a frittata, not a cake; 2 tbsp of flour thickens 500 ml, not 2 litres; a 20 cm tin holds about 1.2 litres of batter.

## 2. Seasoning and acid

- Is there salt at all, and is it added in layers (vegetables as they soften, meat before browning, the sauce at the end)?
- Is there an acid at the end (lemon, vinegar, wine, yoghurt, tomato)? Most "bland" complaints are missing acid, not missing salt.
- Is there a sweetness balance where acidity is high (tomato sauces: a pinch of sugar or a carrot)?
- Is there pepper or heat, and fresh herbs added at the end rather than boiled for an hour?
- Umami: stock, parmesan, anchovy, soy, miso, tomato purée, mushrooms, when the dish tastes thin.
- Fat: a knob of butter or a glug of oil at the end rounds a sauce; a dish with no fat tastes harsh.

## 3. Method order

- Mise en place before heat for anything fast (stir-fries, pan sauces, risotto).
- Browning before braising: meat and onions coloured before liquid, in batches, in a hot pan; the fond deglazed.
- Aromatics (garlic, spices) after onions have softened and for under a minute before liquid, or they burn.
- Dairy and eggs tempered or added off the heat; cornflour slaked in cold liquid.
- Resting: steaks 5 minutes, roasts 15 to 30, bread 1 hour before cutting, cakes 10 minutes in the tin then out.
- Carry-over: large roasts rise 3 to 5 °C after leaving the oven; take them out early.
- Pasta finished in the sauce with pasta water, not sauced on the plate.
- Dough rests: pastry 30 minutes chilled before rolling and again before baking; bread proved until a dent springs back slowly.
- Order of a multi-component dish: the longest item first, the garnish last, and a line saying what can be done ahead.

## 4. Timings and temperatures: plausibility

- Oven temperatures stated as conventional and fan. "Moderate oven" is 180 °C; "hot" 220 °C; "slow" 150 °C.
- Roast chicken 1.5 kg: 1 hour 20 at 200 °C plus rest. Beef joint: 20 min per 500 g at 180 °C plus 20 for medium. Pork shoulder pulled: 5 to 6 hours at 150 °C. Lamb shoulder slow: 4 hours at 160 °C. Whole fish 1 kg: 25 to 30 minutes at 200 °C.
- Braises: beef chuck 2½ to 3 hours at a bare simmer or 150 °C; lamb shanks 2½ hours; chicken thighs 45 minutes. "Simmer 20 minutes" for stewing beef is a finding.
- Dried pulses: 1 to 2 hours after soaking; "20 minutes" is tinned.
- Caramelised onions 30 to 45 minutes, not 5. Softened onions 8 to 10.
- Cakes: 20 cm sponge 25 to 30 minutes at 180 °C; loaf cake 50 to 60 at 170 °C; brownies 25 to 30 at 180 °C. Cookies 10 to 12 at 180 °C.
- Bread: 30 to 40 minutes at 220 to 230 °C for a 500 g loaf; internal 93 to 96 °C.
- Rice absorption: 10 to 12 minutes plus 10 standing. Pasta 1 minute under the packet time.
- Eggs: soft 6 minutes, jammy 7, hard 9 to 10 from boiling.
- Grilling and frying times under 2 minutes for anything thicker than 1 cm are implausible; over 10 minutes a side for a steak is a finding.

## 5. Doneness cues

Every cooking step should have a cue the user can see, hear, smell or measure, not only a time:

- Poultry 75 °C at the bone (USDA 74 °C); pork 75 °C (USDA 63 °C plus rest); mince 75 °C; fish 63 °C or flaking; reheated food 75 °C steaming.
- Steaks by temperature: rare 50 to 52, medium rare 55 to 57, medium 60 to 63.
- Cakes: skewer clean, edges shrinking from the tin, springs back to a touch.
- Bread: hollow sound, 93 to 96 °C, deep brown crust.
- Custards: coats the back of a spoon, 82 to 84 °C; baked custards wobble like a jelly at the centre.
- Caramel by colour and temperature (see the techniques reference); jam 105 °C or wrinkles on a cold plate.
- Onions: translucent (softened), golden (sweated longer), deep brown (caramelised).
- Sauces: coats a spoon and a line drawn through holds; reduced "by half" measured against the pan side.
- Dough: windowpane; proved when a dent springs back slowly.

## 6. Equipment and quantities of equipment

- Tin size stated and matched to the batter volume (20 cm round ≈ 1.2 l; 23 cm ≈ 1.6 l; 900 g loaf ≈ 1.5 l; 20 cm square ≈ 1.6 l).
- Pan size matched to the quantity (500 g of mince needs a 28 cm pan or batches).
- Lidded pot, ovenproof handle, thermometer, sieve, stand mixer: say what is needed and what to do without it.
- Oven shelf position and whether the dish is covered.

## 7. Allergens

Flag the regulated allergens present (gluten, milk, egg, nuts, peanuts, soya, fish, crustaceans, molluscs, sesame, celery, mustard, sulphites, lupin), including hidden sources (soy sauce, stock cubes, Worcestershire sauce, pesto). One line; do not adapt unless asked.

## 8. Servings and scaling

- Does the stated serving count match the quantities (see meat and pasta per person above)?
- If the recipe was scaled by the user, were seasoning, leavening and pan size handled (recipe-scaling skill)?
- Do the sub-recipes (sauce, topping, filling) add up to the main recipe's size?

## 9. Vague instructions

Replace: "a knob of butter" (20 to 30 g), "a glug of oil" (1 to 2 tbsp), "a handful" (30 to 40 g leaves, 50 g nuts), "a pinch" (⅛ tsp), "a hot oven" (220 °C), "medium heat" (describe the pan: oil shimmering), "cook until done" (cue and temperature), "season to taste" (a starting amount, then taste), "simmer" (small bubbles breaking the surface), "a cup" (state the system: 240 ml US, 250 ml metric), "a tin of tomatoes" (400 g).

## 10. Safety findings that override everything else

- Home canning or bottling of low-acid foods (vegetables, meat, stock, most sauces) without a pressure-canning process: botulism risk; water-bath canning is only for high-acid foods with a tested recipe.
- Garlic or herbs in oil stored at room temperature: botulism risk; refrigerate and use within 4 days, or acidify by a tested method.
- Raw or lightly cooked egg (mousse, tiramisu, mayonnaise, royal icing) for pregnant people, under fives, over sixty-fives or anyone immunocompromised, unless the eggs carry a national safe-egg mark.
- Poultry, pork or mince with timings that cannot reach 75 °C; stuffed birds without a stuffing temperature.
- Dried kidney beans without a 10-minute hard boil.
- Cooling instructions that leave food at room temperature for more than 2 hours, or "leave the rice out overnight".
- Reheating more than once; reheating rice after more than a day.
- Raw flour or raw dough eaten (cookie dough) without heat-treating the flour.
- Rhubarb leaves, raw elderberries, uncooked bitter almonds, improperly prepared cassava or ackee, wild mushrooms without expert identification.
- Alcohol "cooked off": 10 to 50 percent remains after typical cooking; flag for children and anyone avoiding alcohol.

## Failure diagnosis

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bland stew, soup or sauce | under-salted; no acid; no umami; herbs boiled away | salt to 1 percent of weight; a squeeze of lemon or a teaspoon of vinegar at the end; stock, parmesan rind or anchovy; fresh herbs at the end |
| Dense, heavy cake | over-mixed after flour; too little leavening; oven too cool; too much flour (scooped cups) | fold flour in gently; check 1 tsp baking powder per 100 to 125 g; weigh flour; check the oven with a thermometer |
| Sunken cake | under-baked; door opened early; too much leavening or sugar; tin too small | bake until the skewer is clean; keep the door shut for the first two thirds; check ratios; use the stated tin |
| Dry cake or muffins | over-baked; too little fat or liquid; too much flour | check 5 minutes earlier; weigh; add 1 to 2 tbsp liquid |
| Tough, chewy cookies or pastry | over-worked gluten; too little fat; warm dough | mix until just combined; rest and chill 30 minutes |
| Soggy pastry base | not blind baked; wet filling added to raw pastry; oven too cool; glass dish | blind bake 15 minutes with beans plus 5 without; brush with egg; bake on a preheated tray at 200 °C |
| Flat bread, no rise | yeast dead or cold kitchen; too much salt or sugar touching yeast; under-proved | test the yeast in warm water with a pinch of sugar; prove longer in a warmer place; 2 percent salt |
| Gummy bread | under-baked; cut while hot | bake to 93 to 96 °C internal; cool 1 hour |
| Split or grainy sauce (hollandaise, custard, cheese sauce) | too hot; fat added too fast; cheese boiled | lower heat; whisk a spoon of warm water into a clean bowl and add the sauce slowly; add cheese off the heat |
| Lumpy sauce or gravy | flour added to hot liquid; not whisked | slake in cold liquid; whisk and sieve |
| Watery sauce or curry | too much liquid; not reduced; vegetables released water | reduce uncovered; cornflour slurry; salt and drain watery vegetables first |
| Dry chicken breast or pork chop | over-cooked; no brine or rest | cook to 74 °C / 63 °C plus rest; brine 30 minutes in 5 percent salt; rest 5 minutes |
| Tough stewing meat | not cooked long enough; boiled hard | bare simmer or 150 °C oven for 2½ to 3 hours; fork test |
| Grey, steamed meat or vegetables | crowded pan; wet surface; pan not hot | pat dry; batches; preheat the pan until the oil shimmers |
| Mushy pasta or rice | over-cooked; too much water for absorption rice; stirred rice | 1 minute under packet time; 1 : 1.5 and do not stir; rinse rice |
| Burnt garlic, bitter dish | garlic added with onions or on high heat | add garlic for the last 30 to 60 seconds before liquid |
| Soggy roast potatoes or vegetables | crowded tray; not dry; oven too cool; oil cold | preheat the oil and the tray at 200 to 220 °C; single layer; parboil and rough up potatoes; dry thoroughly |
| Rubbery eggs or omelette | too hot, too long | low heat for scrambled, off the heat glossy; omelette folded while soft |
| Curdled cream in a soup or curry | boiled after adding; acidic base | add cream off the heat or use double cream; temper yoghurt with a spoon of hot liquid and a pinch of cornflour |
| Fried food greasy | oil too cool; batches too large | 170 to 190 °C; small batches; drain on a rack |
| Crystallised caramel or fudge | stirred; sugar on the pan sides | do not stir once boiling; brush sides with water; add a little glucose or lemon |
| Jam not setting | not enough pectin or acid; not boiled long enough | 105 °C or wrinkle test; add lemon juice; use jam sugar for low-pectin fruit |
| Meringue weeping or chewy | under-dissolved sugar; humid day; under-baked | add sugar slowly until no grit; bake low and long; cool in the oven |
| Pancakes or batters tough | over-mixed; no rest | mix until just combined with lumps; rest 20 minutes |
| Stir-fry soggy | wok not hot enough; too much in the pan; vegetables wet | smoking hot pan; batches; dry ingredients; sauce last |
| Pulses still hard after hours | old beans; acid or salt added early; hard water | buy fresh beans; add tomato and salt after they soften; a pinch of bicarbonate in the soaking water |
