# Techniques: principles, cues, mistakes

## Heat and browning

- **Maillard browning** starts above about 140 °C on a dry surface. Pat meat and vegetables dry, do not crowd the pan (steam drops the temperature), use enough fat, and leave food alone until it releases itself. Cue: the piece lifts without sticking. Mistake: moving it early; adding cold food to a lukewarm pan.
- **Searing does not seal in juices**; it builds flavour. Rest meat after cooking so the juices redistribute: 5 minutes for a steak, 15–30 for a roast.
- **Caramelising onions** takes 30–45 minutes on medium-low with a pinch of salt; "caramelised in 5 minutes" is browned, not caramelised. Add a splash of water to lift the fond when it darkens.
- **Deep frying**: 170–190 °C; small batches; drain on a rack, not paper. Cue: bubbles slow and the sound quietens when the water has cooked out.
- **Roasting vegetables**: 200–220 °C, cut evenly, oil and salt, single layer, turn once. Cue: edges dark and crisp.

## Sauces and emulsions

- **Emulsions** (mayonnaise, hollandaise, vinaigrette, beurre blanc): add fat slowly into a small amount of liquid with constant whisking; the first third matters most. Rescue a split sauce by starting again with a spoon of warm water and whisking the split sauce back in.
- **Pan sauce**: pour off fat, deglaze with wine or stock, scrape the fond, reduce, mount with cold butter off the heat.
- **Roux**: equal weights butter and flour, cooked 2 minutes for white, 10–20 for blond and brown; add liquid warm, whisk, simmer at least 10 minutes to cook out the flour.
- **Cornflour**: slake in cold liquid, add at the end, do not boil for long or it thins.
- **Reduction** concentrates salt and sugar: season at the end.
- **Tomato sauce**: long and slow for depth, or quick and bright; a pinch of sugar corrects acidity; a knob of butter rounds it.

## Stocks, braises and stews

- **Stock**: cold water, bring to a bare simmer, skim, never boil (cloudy), 3–4 hours for chicken, 6–8 for beef, 30–45 minutes for fish and vegetables. Roast bones first for brown stock.
- **Braising**: brown, build the base, add liquid to come halfway up, cover, low oven (150–160 °C) or barely trembling simmer until a fork meets no resistance. Collagen turns to gelatine from about 70 °C over hours; "tough" means "not yet". Cue: the meat falls from a fork.
- **Stews taste better the next day**: flavours even out and fat can be lifted when cold.

## Rice, grains and pulses

- **Absorption-method rice**: rinse until the water runs nearly clear; 1 part rice to 1.5 parts water for long grain (basmati soaked 30 minutes, then 1:1.25); bring to the boil, lid on, lowest heat 10–12 minutes, off the heat 10 minutes, fork through.
- **Risotto**: toast the rice in fat, add hot stock a ladle at a time, stir often but not constantly, 17–18 minutes, finish off the heat with butter and cheese (mantecatura); it should flow on the plate.
- **Dried beans**: soak 8–12 hours (or quick-soak: boil 2 minutes, stand 1 hour), simmer gently with aromatics, salt in the last half hour, 1–2 hours depending on age. Kidney beans must boil hard for 10 minutes first.
- **Lentils** need no soaking; red 15–20 minutes to collapse, green and brown 25–30 holding shape, Puy and beluga 20–25.
- **Pasta**: 1 litre water and 10 g salt per 100 g pasta; cook 1 minute under the packet time, finish in the sauce with a splash of pasta water (the starch binds the sauce).

## Eggs

- **Boiled**: into boiling water from the fridge, 6 min soft, 7 jammy, 9–10 hard; straight into iced water.
- **Poached**: fresh eggs, water at a bare simmer, a splash of vinegar optional, 3 minutes; strain the thin white first for a neat shape.
- **Scrambled**: low heat, constant stirring, off the heat while still glossy; they carry on cooking.
- **Omelette**: hot pan, butter foaming, stir and shake 20 seconds, fold while the centre is still soft.
- **Custard**: 82–84 °C, coats the back of a spoon, never boils; strain.
- **Meringue**: clean bowl, whites at room temperature, add sugar once soft peaks form, a tablespoon at a time; stiff and glossy, no grit when rubbed.

## Bread and pastry

- **Bread**: hydration decides crumb (60 % tight, 75 % open); knead until the windowpane test passes or use folds over time; first rise until doubled; shape; prove until a poked dent springs back slowly; bake hot (230–250 °C) with steam for the first 15 minutes. Cue: internal 93–96 °C, hollow sound.
- **Shortcrust**: cold fat rubbed into flour to breadcrumbs, minimal cold water, rest 30 minutes, blind bake with beans at 190 °C for 15 minutes then 5 without.
- **Rough puff**: cold butter in large flakes, four book folds with rests; bake at 200–220 °C.
- **Choux**: cook the flour into the boiling water and butter until it forms a ball, cool slightly, beat in eggs until it falls from the spoon in a V; bake without opening the oven.
- **Cakes (creaming)**: butter and sugar beaten pale, eggs one at a time (curdling means too cold or too fast; add a spoon of flour), flour folded in gently, oven middle shelf, test with a skewer. Do not open the door in the first two thirds of the bake.
- **Tempering chocolate** (seed method): melt two thirds to 45–50 °C dark (40–45 milk and white), add the remaining third chopped, stir until 27–28 °C, warm to 31–32 °C dark (29–30 milk, 28–29 white). Cue: a smear sets glossy in 3–5 minutes at room temperature.

## Knife skills and prep

- Pinch grip on the blade, claw hand on the food, tip stays on the board for a rocking cut. A sharp knife is safer than a blunt one.
- Standard cuts: brunoise 2–3 mm dice, small dice 6 mm, medium 12 mm; julienne matchsticks; chiffonade for leaves (roll and slice).
- Onion: halve through the root, peel, horizontal cuts then vertical then across, keeping the root to hold it together.
- Garlic: crush with the flat of the knife to loosen the skin; a pinch of salt turns it to paste.
- Herbs: roll soft herbs and slice once; chopping repeatedly bruises them.
- Mise en place: everything measured and cut before the heat goes on; it is the single biggest upgrade for a home cook.

## Stir-frying and high heat

- Wok or widest pan, smoking hot, oil with a high smoke point, ingredients cut small and uniform, cook in batches, aromatics last 30 seconds, sauce at the end, serve at once. Velveting meat (cornflour, egg white, 20 minutes) keeps it tender.

## Grilling and barbecue

- Two zones: direct heat for searing, indirect for finishing. Lid down for anything over 2 cm thick. Rest. Clean, oiled grates stop sticking. Charcoal is ready when covered in grey ash.

## Seasoning

- Salt in layers, not all at the end; taste at each stage. Acid (lemon, vinegar) brightens a flat dish faster than more salt. A pinch of sugar balances acidity. Fresh herbs at the end, dried at the start. Toast whole spices 30–60 seconds before grinding.
