# Appliance conversions

All times are starting points. Check at the earlier end and use the doneness cue.

## Oven to air fryer

- Temperature: reduce by 15 to 20 °C (200 °C oven → 180 °C air fryer). Time: reduce by about 20 percent (40 min → 30 to 32 min). Shake the basket or turn halfway.
- Single layer only. Overlapping food steams. Cook in batches and reheat the first batch for 2 minutes at the end.
- Oil lightly: a teaspoon or a spray; too much drips and smokes. Season after oiling so it sticks.
- No wet batter (it drips through the basket before setting); use a dry coating of flour then egg then crumbs. No loose parchment or foil until food is on top of it.
- Preheat 3 minutes for anything that should crisp. Fatty foods (sausages, bacon, chicken skin) need no added oil; add 2 tbsp water under the basket to reduce smoke.
- Bakes: small tins only (a 15 to 18 cm tin in most drawers), 160 °C, cover with foil if the top browns before the centre sets.

## Oven or hob to slow cooker

- Equivalents: oven at 150 to 160 °C for 2 hours ≈ slow cooker LOW 7 to 8 hours ≈ HIGH 3 to 4 hours. A hob simmer of 45 minutes ≈ LOW 4 to 6 hours ≈ HIGH 2 to 3 hours.
- Brown meat and soften onions in a pan first (or in the cooker's sear function). Deglaze the pan into the pot.
- Reduce liquid by about a third: nothing evaporates. The pot should be between half and two thirds full.
- Add in the last 30 to 60 minutes: dairy and cream, pasta, rice, quick-cooking vegetables (peas, spinach, courgette, peppers), fresh herbs, cornflour slurry to thicken.
- Root vegetables cook slower than meat in a slow cooker: cut small and put them at the bottom, under the meat.
- Do not lift the lid; each lift costs 15 to 20 minutes. Do not use the keep-warm setting for more than 2 hours for food safety.
- Not suited to: anything that needs a crust, quick fish, rare meat, most bakes. Suited to: braises, pulled meat, pulses, stocks, porridge overnight, poached fruit.
- Finish under the grill or in a hot oven for 10 minutes if browning matters (gratins, pulled pork).

## Oven or hob to pressure cooker (stovetop or Instant Pot at high pressure)

- Add 10 to 15 minutes to reach pressure and 10 to 20 for a natural release; a "15 minute" recipe is 40 minutes door to door.
- Minimum liquid 250 ml (check the model). Fill no more than two thirds; half for beans, grains, pasta and anything that foams.
- Thick sauces scorch: layer tomato purée and dairy on top without stirring, or add them after cooking. Thicken after cooking with the lid off on the sauté setting.
- Release: natural (NR) for meat, pulses, grains, stock and foaming liquids; quick (QR) for vegetables, fish, pasta; 10 minutes natural then quick (10NR) for rice and tougher vegetables.
- A pressure cooker browns nothing. Sear first, finish under the grill if a crust matters.

## Oven to microwave, and microwave-only kitchens

- Microwaves heat water; they do not brown. Good for: steaming vegetables (100 g, 1 tbsp water, covered, 2 to 4 min), fish fillets (covered, 2 to 3 min per 150 g), poaching eggs (in water, pierce the yolk, 45 to 60 s), rice (absorption method, 1:2, 10 min high then 10 min low or standing), porridge, melting chocolate and butter (30 s bursts), softening onions (3 to 4 min with a knob of butter), jacket potatoes (pierce, 8 to 10 min for a large one, then 10 min in a hot oven or air fryer for a crisp skin if available), mug cakes (a single portion, 90 s to 2 min at 800 W, let stand), reheating.
- Cover and vent; stir or turn halfway; count standing time (food keeps cooking for 1 to 2 minutes).
- Power: times here are for 800 to 900 W. At 700 W add 15 percent; at 1000 W take off 10 percent.
- Not for: anything that must crisp, large joints, deep-frying, bread with a crust, eggs in their shells, sealed jars, metal, whole chillies (the steam burns).
- A microwave plus a toaster or a sandwich press covers browning for toasties, quesadillas and flatbreads.

## No oven: hob-only baking and roasting

- Flatbreads: any dough in a dry, very hot frying pan, 1 to 2 minutes a side (pitta puffs with the lid on). Soda bread as a skillet loaf, lid on, low heat, 25 to 30 minutes, flipped once.
- Pan cakes: a 20 cm nonstick pan with a lid, lowest heat, batter no more than 2 cm deep, 25 to 35 minutes; or a heat diffuser under a cake tin inside a large lidded pot (the pot becomes an oven, about 160 to 170 °C). Dutch-oven baking over a hob works the same way with a trivet.
- "Baked" potatoes: parboil 10 minutes, then crisp in a covered pan with oil, 20 minutes, turning.
- Roast chicken alternative: spatchcock and pan-roast skin down with a weight, 15 minutes, turn, lid on, 20 to 25 minutes; or poach then crisp the skin in a pan.
- Roast vegetables alternative: pan-roast in a wide pan, lid on for 10 minutes to soften, lid off and high heat to char.
- Gratins and crumbles: cook the filling on the hob, top with a pan-toasted crumb or crumble mix.
- Steamed puddings and sponges: a basin in a pot of simmering water, 90 minutes to 2 hours.

## Hob types

- Induction: only ferromagnetic pans (a magnet sticks); heats and cools instantly, so start one setting lower than gas; the pan must be flat and centred or it cuts out; wok cooking needs an induction wok ring or a flat-bottomed wok.
- Electric solid plates: slow to respond; move the pan off the plate to stop cooking.
- Gas: flame should not lick the sides of the pan; a wok works well.
- Single hotplate: cook in sequence, keep the first dish covered under a tea towel, and favour one-pot dishes.

## Barbecue and grill

- Two zones: coals banked to one side (or one burner off). Sear over direct heat, finish over indirect with the lid down. Lid down for anything thicker than 2 cm.
- Chicken pieces: direct 3 to 4 minutes a side to colour, then indirect 25 to 35 minutes to 75 °C. Whole joints: indirect only, lid down, as an oven at 160 to 180 °C.
- Vegetables: direct heat, oiled, in a basket or on skewers. Fish: oiled skin on a very clean grate or in a foil parcel.
- Coals are ready when grey with ash, 20 to 30 minutes after lighting. Hand test above the grate: 2 to 3 seconds is hot, 5 to 6 medium.
- Bread, pizza and flatbreads work on a stone or heavy tray with the lid down.

## Halogen and combi ovens

- Halogen: fast and fierce from above; reduce the oven temperature by 20 °C and check 25 percent earlier; use the lower rack and the extender ring for tall food; cover with foil for the first half of long roasts.
- Combi (microwave plus convection): use convection alone for anything that browns; combination modes shorten jacket potatoes and roasts by about a third; follow the manufacturer's table for the microwave share.

## Tiny, student and camping kitchens

- Favour: one-pot pasta (pasta cooked in its sauce with 500 ml liquid per 200 g), tray and pan meals, stir-fries, omelettes, grain bowls, pulses from tins, noodle soups.
- Prepare at home for camping: marinate meat, pre-chop vegetables, pre-mix pancake dry ingredients, freeze a stew to cook as it thaws and to keep the cool box cold.
- One good pan with a lid, a sharp knife and a board do more than any gadget.
