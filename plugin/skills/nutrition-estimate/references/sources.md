# Sources and caveats for nutrition-table.csv

Values are per 100 g of the ingredient as bought (raw unless the name says cooked or tinned), rounded to typical published figures. They were compiled from the ranges in the UK Composition of Foods Integrated Dataset (McCance and Widdowson, Public Health England, open government licence) and the USDA FoodData Central Foundation and SR Legacy datasets (public domain). Where the two differ, a middle value was taken. Brands, cuts, trimming, ripeness and cooking method all move the real figures, often by 10 to 20 percent.

Columns: name, kcal, protein_g, fat_g, saturates_g, carbs_g, sugars_g, fibre_g, salt_g (sodium × 2.5), category.

Conventions:

- Meat and fish are raw, edible portion, with skin or visible fat unless the name says otherwise ("chicken thigh skinless", "lean beef mince").
- Grains, pasta and pulses have "dry" and "cooked" entries because recipes weigh them either way; cooked weights are about 2.2 to 2.5 times the dry weight for rice and pasta and about 2.5 for lentils.
- Tinned pulses are drained weight.
- Spices, dried herbs and fresh herbs are generic entries; their contribution to a recipe is small.
- "stock" is made-up liquid stock; "stock cube" is the dry cube.
- Alcohol: kcal include the alcohol itself (7 kcal per gram); carbohydrate is the residual sugar.
- Salt for unsalted fresh foods is given as 0.01 to 0.2 g to reflect natural sodium.

Caveats the skill must state:

- Everything is an estimate. Cooking changes weight (water loss concentrates nutrients per gram; absorbed oil adds fat).
- Counted items use typical weights (one egg 50 g, one onion 150 g). Big onions and small eggs exist.
- Volumes use typical densities; a "cup of flour" varies by a third depending on how it is scooped.
- Nothing here is medical or dietetic advice.
