# Pairing guide

## Principles

- **Weight to weight.** Light dishes with light drinks; rich with full. A Pinot Grigio drowns under beef stew; a Barolo flattens steamed sole.
- **Acid.** A drink should be at least as acidic as the dish or it tastes flat. Tomato, citrus and vinaigrette want high acid (Sauvignon Blanc, Chianti, dry Riesling). Acid also cuts fat (Champagne with fried food).
- **Sweetness.** The drink must be as sweet as or sweeter than the food, or it turns sour: desserts need dessert wines; sweet glazes and hoisin like off-dry whites or fruity reds.
- **Tannin.** Tannin (Cabernet, Nebbiolo, Syrah, strong black tea) softens with protein and fat, so red meat and hard cheese. Tannin against chilli or fish tastes metallic and harsh.
- **Spice heat.** Chilli amplifies alcohol and oak. Choose lower alcohol, a touch of sweetness, no oak: off-dry Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Beaujolais, lager, wheat beer, lassi.
- **Salt** makes wine taste rounder and fruitier; salty dishes (cured meat, feta, anchovy) can take sharper or more tannic drinks.
- **Umami** (mushrooms, soy, aged cheese, miso) makes wine taste bitter and thin; choose umami-rich drinks (sake, aged Champagne, sherry, red Burgundy) or add salt and acid to the dish.
- **Bitter** foods (radicchio, charred greens) want fruit and a little sweetness, not tannin.
- **Regional** pairings are a safe default: what grows together goes together (Muscadet with oysters, Rioja with lamb, Sangiovese with tomato pasta).

## Serving

- Sparkling and light whites 6–8 °C, full whites and rosé 8–12 °C, light reds 12–14 °C, full reds 16–18 °C (cooler than most rooms).
- Decant young tannic reds for an hour; most other wines need only opening.
- Beer: lagers cold, ales cool, stouts cellar temperature.
- Alcohol-free: serve in a proper glass with ice and a garnish; temperature and bubbles do a lot of the work.
